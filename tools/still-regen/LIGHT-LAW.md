# The Light Law (Marco, 2026-07-18)

Marco is still not happy with the stills. Two rulings, both binding on the next
regen pass — and there WILL be a next regen pass: every property's stills get
done once more under these laws.

## 1. Prompts come from the script. The script is the source of truth.

The prompts written to date (largely Grok in Cursor) nearly always sway from
what is written in the script, the treatment, or Marco's stated intent. That
ends. The authoring order for every shot prompt is:

1. **Script first.** Find the scene. Quote the lines the frame is built from
   in the spec, next to the prompt, so drift is visible at review.
2. **Treatment second**, only where the script has no page for the moment.
3. **Nothing invented third.** If neither document supports the shot, it goes
   to Marco as a question, not into a prompt as a guess.

A prompt that cannot cite its scene does not run.

## 2. The light must be found, not rigged.

The current frames are lit too perfectly — you can practically see the lighting
units just off frame. **L.A. Dolce Vita is the worst offender**, but the ruling
is all-around, every property:

- **Natural light is best.** Sun, window, sky, practical fixtures that belong
  to the location. Own the light the place actually has (this composes with
  the Limelight standard: name the real place, own ITS light).
- **Less Hollywood.** No three-point glamour, no motivated-from-nowhere rims,
  no perfectly filled shadows, no golden-hour-on-command. If a frame looks
  like a unit publicity still, it is wrong.
- Imperfection is the tell of reality: blown windows, hard noon sun, mixed
  color temperatures, faces that fall into shadow. A stranger should read the
  frame as *found*, not *staged*.

## Composition of laws

Story Law owns WHY the frame exists. Limelight owns HOW it is made.
The Light Law owns WHERE the words and the light come from: the words from
the script, the light from the location. All three apply to every shot.
