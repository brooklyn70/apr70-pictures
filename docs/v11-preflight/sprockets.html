First, the thing I got wrong

This morning you said the marquee lights "should be svg circles", and I built circles. You have now looked at them and you do not like them. You are right, and the reason is that a real sprocket hole is not a circle.

35mm perforations are rounded rectangles, wider than they are tall. That is the Kodak KS geometry, and it is what a projectionist sees when the gate is open. And the tell is already in your own house: the APR 70 wordmark's sprockets are <rect> elements, and there is a .sprocket rule sitting unused in tokens.css at 13×9 with a corner radius. The brand already knew.

Whatever shape you pick, one thing must change regardless: the holes are currently painted var(--bg-0), the page background, which is why they vanish in Marquee Night. Film is backlit. A perforation shows the lamp behind the stock, never the wall behind the screen. The hole colour is a constant of the film, not a variable of the page.

A · circles, as shipped the defect

True circles, holes painted the page background. In House Lights they read. In Marquee Night they are black on black. Switch the mode and watch them disappear.

B · the true 35mm perforation recommended

Rounded rectangle, wider than tall, light in both modes. This is what film actually looks like, it matches the sprockets in your own wordmark, and it is the shape the unused .sprocket rule in the codebase was already describing.

C · the wordmark's own sprocket

The exact geometry of the logo: a small, near-square rect, tighter pitch. The most literal possible tie between the mark and the chrome. Quieter than B, and it reads more as a row of ticks than as film.

D · the perforation, with the lamp behind it

B, plus a warm bloom. This is the only option that earns the phrase "marquee lights" ... the others are holes; this one is lit. Costs a single drop-shadow. It is also the loudest, and on a page of nine photographs it may be one flourish too many.

E · no holes

The film bed with a hairline gate and nothing punched. Worth seeing, because the honest question is whether the rails earn their place at all. You killed the decorative rails in v9 yourself ... "added fluff and do nothing to tell the story of who we are."

F · the full 35mm edge exists already

Not mocked up, because it is real code sitting in the legacy monolith: _legacy-APR_70/src/components/FilmstripTop.tsx. A cream film base, a dark keycode data band printed EASTMAN 5243 025 02401 191 ID with footage counters and tiny keycode glyphs rendered upside down as on real stock, a teal glow line, and perforations that regenerate on resize to fill any monitor (capped at 900 holes, commented "Marco: 50 inch monitors").

It is the most beautiful piece of chrome this project has ever had and it is also, almost certainly, the thing you meant when you said "decorative rails". Say the word and I will port it. But be warned: it is a lot, and it is the direction v9 explicitly walked away from.
