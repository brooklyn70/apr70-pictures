### 3. Lines That Still Fail

**Home / sections.2.body AFTER:**
"Here the story does the heavy lifting. Drama over information. Subtext over statement. A face held long enough to matter."

This is the script lecturing the audience about what drama is. It is telling us the rules instead of showing them. The exact corn Capra spent a career fighting. The audience does not need to be told; they need to be shown. Fix: replace with a specific image — a face on screen held without cutting, four seconds, no cut — or cut it entirely and let the loglines do the work.

**Craft / sections.1.body AFTER:**
"…as we build a studio for narratives of moral complexity, human resilience, and lasting impact: heroism with open eyes, ambiguity with a conscience…"

Three abstract nouns doing the job of a single concrete claim. "Moral complexity" is not a picture. "A man who holds on to his decency when everything around him is selling out" is a picture. Fix: replace the three-adjective list with something specific — a person, a place, a choice.

**Mayors / layout.0.body AFTER:**
"…from the American Dream to socialist experiment."

This is a political verdict, not a story. The show claims to be forensic, not hysterical, but this line is hysterical. It tells the audience what to think instead of showing them the evidence. Fix: "What happened to the city they built?" — let the audience draw the conclusion. The autopsy report does the work; the adjective does not.

**Home / sections.7.body AFTER:**
"2026 and 2027 belong to the studios that meet people where they already are, carrying work worth their attention."

"Belong to" — who says? "Worth their attention" — who decides? This is pitch-deck bravado from a studio with ten properties, three of which are drafted. The fact is in the same paragraph: "The slate develops in public view on this site." The fact is enough. Fix: cut the claim. Let the slate speak.

### 4. The One Ruling

Every claim on the site must be verifiable on the page itself. If it cannot be verified, cut it. The loglines are verified by the scripts. The disclosure is verified by the labels. The quotes are verified by the source. The rest — "the classics never go out of style," "the greatest art is born inside clear boundaries" — these are beliefs, not facts. Put them where they belong: as quotes from the touchstones, not as the studio's own claims about itself. The audience will believe the work. They will not believe the wrapper. Show them the work, then get out of the way.
