**WHAT THE REWRITE GOT RIGHT**

The em-dash purge is the single best structural decision in the pass. The BEFORE copy leaned on the dash the way a weak scene leans on a crane shot — motion substituting for decision. The AFTER text breaks those pivots into full stops, and the prose gains the thing the founder's voice actually runs on: the hard stop. "The dominoes were falling from the opening frame" lands harder after three flat sentences than it ever did after a breathless dash-chain.

Second: the *Sea Gate* synopsis. BEFORE was one 130-word sentence with a dash doing load-bearing work at the climax. AFTER breaks it into beats — "His last move makes the wall eat itself." Full stop. That is the picture's third act in nine words, and it now reads as a verdict instead of a breath.

Third: *The Mayors*. "A Ric Burns homage, but not so liberal" kept its comma-splice swagger, "gone with the Dodgers and Luna Park" replaced the simile-adjacent "like the Dodgers" — which also quietly satisfied LAW 1. Whoever caught that double violation earned their chair.

**THE GESTURE**

The one image worth the price of admission is already on the site, and it is not a frame — it is the Founding Roll. "Ask to be named and the next number is yours. The numbers start low, stay yours, and are never reissued." That is the bravura swing: a studio with nothing shipped asking the audience to sign the call sheet before the picture exists, and promising the number is theirs forever. Protect it. The AFTER correctly broke its one long sentence into the counted rhythm, and nothing smaller should be allowed near it.

**THE ONE RULING MARCO HAS TO MAKE**

Decide whether the site is allowed to say "we" at all. The copy insists everywhere that APR 70 is one man — "Every role here is currently filled by the writer" — and then speaks in first person plural on every page: "We bring that discipline back," "We list the facts," "our best work." Either the plural is a deliberate gesture, the studio as a house the reader is being invited into, in which case it must be consistent and never explained — or it is a reflex carried over from pitch-deck habit, in which case the strongest pages (Contact, Methods) prove the singular is better: "The reply comes from the writer" is the best line on the whole site precisely because it is one man standing still. A picture cannot have two protagonists. Neither can a voice. Choose, and make the pronoun load-bearing everywhere or nowhere.
